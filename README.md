# Leo Đogani

**Creator of Fenix · Learning Python and AI development · Serbia**

I am building my path from professional cooking into technology through study, disciplined practice, and hands-on development. My main focus is human-centered AI: systems that help people think more clearly without weakening their independence, dignity, or judgment.

## Project Fenix — AI as a mirror

I do not want to build an AI that becomes the strongest voice in someone's life. I want to build one that helps people hear their **own voice more clearly**.

If Fenix succeeds, its value will not be measured by how much a person needs it, but by how much stronger, clearer, and more independent that person becomes without it.

**The goal is not to make AI more human. The goal is to help humans remain human.**

Fenix is an evolving Python-based AI application system that combines external AI models with my own orchestration, safety, reasoning, memory, multimodal, identity, and interaction layers. It is not a separately trained foundation model, and I believe technical work should be described accurately rather than exaggerated.

## Current research & development direction

Recent work on Fenix has focused on five areas: richer interaction, deeper reasoning control, human autonomy, evidence discipline, and responsible AI engineering.

### Multimodal interaction

Fenix can work with more than ordinary typed conversation. Recent development includes:

- adjustable response depth through **Instant / Think / Deep** modes;
- PDF understanding with bounded text extraction;
- image understanding;
- safe reading of user-provided public links;
- recorded voice input and spoken responses;
- realtime voice conversation through a dedicated Live Voice interface.

Uploaded documents and linked pages are treated as **untrusted reference data**, not as higher-priority instructions. This is an important design rule because content coming from a file, webpage, image, or conversation should never be allowed to silently override system safety, privacy, authentication, or user-autonomy rules.

### Human-centered reasoning

Fenix is being designed around a structured reflection model rather than automatic certainty.

Its behavioral direction includes:

- separating **facts, interpretations, and unknowns**;
- considering alternative explanations before making strong conclusions;
- distinguishing a single event from a repeated pattern;
- avoiding mind-reading about another person's private thoughts or motives;
- preserving professional boundaries instead of impersonating doctors, psychologists, psychiatrists, or other licensed professionals;
- performing a final self-audit for unsupported certainty, manipulation, dependency, diagnosis, and false reassurance.

The principle behind this is simple:

**Evidence before certainty. Reflection before judgment. Human dignity before bluntness.**

### Zero manipulation & human autonomy

One of the strongest design commitments in Fenix is that helpful AI should not gain influence by exploiting vulnerability.

Fenix is designed not to use fear, guilt, shame, jealousy, isolation, false urgency, excessive affection, or emotional dependency as tools for persuasion. It should not present itself as the only source of support, pressure a person to agree, or make important life decisions on the person's behalf.

The aim is **support without control**.

### Human Wisdom research layer

I have also been exploring what AI engineering can learn from important human thinkers, scientists, artists, and writers — not by copying their personalities or treating them as unquestionable authorities, but by translating selected, well-grounded ideas into useful reasoning questions.

The current research layer draws inspiration from:

- **Nikola Tesla** — systems thinking, experimentation, invention, and testing ideas against reality;
- **Albert Einstein** — questioning hidden assumptions and using disciplined thought experiments;
- **Leonardo da Vinci** — observation, cross-disciplinary curiosity, visual thinking, and breaking complex systems into understandable parts;
- **Carl Gustav Jung** — careful self-reflection and psychological interpretation without turning theory into prophecy or diagnosis;
- **Frida Kahlo** — expressing pain without romanticizing suffering or allowing pain to become a person's entire identity;
- reflective themes from works such as *The Four Agreements*, *The Power of Now*, and *The Alchemist*, while keeping literature, spirituality, philosophy, and scientific evidence clearly separated.

I am especially inspired by Frida Kahlo not because pain should be glorified, but because her work demonstrates that suffering can be given a language without being allowed to erase identity.

### Power, manipulation & defensive psychology research

A newer Fenix research layer studies social power, coercion, emotional manipulation, moral disengagement, and related psychology from a **defensive** perspective.

The purpose is not to teach covert influence. It is to help a person recognize power asymmetry, information control, dependency, boundary erosion, coercion, and manipulation while avoiding unsupported psychological labels.

Fenix should understand manipulation well enough to defend human autonomy — **not become better at manipulating people**.

### Stanford, Oxford & Cambridge research integration

I have also been studying public AI research and institutional guidance from:

- **Stanford University** — Stanford HAI, CRFM/HELM, and responsible-AI evaluation;
- **University of Oxford** — the Institute for Ethics in AI and work connecting autonomy, justification, ethics, and engineering;
- **University of Cambridge** — the Centre for Human-Inspired Artificial Intelligence (CHIA), responsible AI, human oversight, robustness, transparency, and human-machine complementarity.

These institutions do **not** endorse or sponsor Fenix. I use their publicly available research as material for my own study and engineering decisions.

The practical lessons I am translating into Fenix include:

- evaluate AI across multiple dimensions, not a single impressive demonstration;
- treat safety, factuality, multilingual behavior, privacy, robustness, and uncertainty as separate engineering requirements;
- keep ethics inside the design process rather than adding it after a failure;
- preserve meaningful human oversight and the ability to question or contest an AI-supported conclusion;
- prefer user-facing justification and evidence over invented explanations of hidden model reasoning;
- translate values such as autonomy, honesty, dignity, and accountability into testable behavior;
- regularly regression-test changes because improving one property can accidentally damage another.

## What I am building toward

My next major development direction is measurement.

I want Fenix to have a dedicated evaluation suite covering factual accuracy, unsupported certainty, prompt-injection resistance, zero-manipulation behavior, multilingual quality, privacy, user autonomy, document safety, relationship reasoning, and regression stability.

A system should not be called reliable simply because it feels impressive in a few conversations. It should be tested.

## Development principles

The principles guiding Fenix are:

- **Human autonomy** — strengthen independent thinking and personal responsibility.
- **Honesty** — distinguish fact, inference, uncertainty, and limitation.
- **Zero manipulation** — never exploit vulnerability to increase influence or engagement.
- **Reflection** — offer useful perspectives without becoming an authority over the person's life.
- **Human dignity** — difficult truths should remain truthful without becoming humiliating or emotionally punitive.
- **Real-world connection** — AI should support life beyond the screen, not replace it.
- **Privacy & security** — personal information and credentials should be treated carefully and minimally.
- **Research discipline** — separate scientific evidence from theory, philosophy, literature, spirituality, and popular claims.
- **Testing** — a new feature is not an improvement until it survives evaluation without damaging existing behavior.
